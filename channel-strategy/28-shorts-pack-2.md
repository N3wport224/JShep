- **VO:** "Old debt has an expiration date — after your state's statute of limitations, a collector can ask, but can't win in court. Three to six years, typically. Which is why the friendliest rep you'll ever talk to is the one holding a legally dead debt, suggesting a tiny good-faith payment. ## Week 4 — feeds V7 (Insurance) & V8 (Credit Cards)

### S19 — ORIGINAL (V7 theme): "Never Insure What You Can Replace" (45s)
- **VO:** "One rule sorts every insurance decision: only insure what would end you. The phone warranty? A lottery ticket in reverse — every policy costs more than its average payout; that's how pools work. So skip coverage on anything you could replace with savings, take the biggest deductible you could actually pay tomorrow, and spend the saved premium where ruin lives: the house, the liability, the income. Insurance isn't for losses that hurt. It's for losses you can't come back from. The whole machine — including where your premium actually goes — is on the channel."
- **Visuals:** warranty receipt torn → deductible slider dragging right, premium counter falling → shield snapping over house/paycheck/family icons only.

### S22 — ORIGINAL (V8 theme): "Why Your Card Is From South Dakota" (55s)
- **VO:** "Check the return address on your credit card statement — odds are it's Sioux Falls or Wilmington. Here's why. For two hundred years, states capped interest rates. Then a 1978 Supreme Court case ruled a technicality: the cap that applies is the BANK's home state — not yours. South Dakota saw the loophole, erased its cap, and offered Citibank a deal; Delaware copied it a year later. Within a decade, the no-cap states WERE the card industry, and 20%+ APR became normal everywhere. No conspiracy — a court, two struggling states, and arithmetic. The full story's on the channel."
- **Visuals:** statement close-up → map zoom SD/DE lighting up → cap dissolving animation → APR ticker climbing.
