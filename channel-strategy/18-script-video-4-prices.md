## ACT 1 — THE HEADLINE TRICK (0:35–3:00)

**VO:** First, the concession: inflation genuinely did slow down. When the news says "inflation is cooling," that's not a lie. It's something sneakier — a switch of units while you weren't looking.
"Inflation" measures how fast prices are *climbing.* It says nothing about how high they already are. So "inflation fell from nine percent to three" means: prices sprinted uphill, and are now… walking uphill. The hill never goes down. The walking *is* the win.

[GFX: mountain-climber on a slope — slope angle eases from steep to gentle, but altitude keeps rising; altimeter ticking up throughout]
[TEXT: "Cooling ≠ falling. Slower ≠ back."]

**VO:** Check the machine's own gauge: the consumer price index — the running total of the climb — is up roughly *twenty-five percent* since twenty-nineteen. And this spring it was still climbing at three point eight percent a year, the fastest in three years. The sprint ended. The stairs remain.

[GFX: CPI index line 2019→2026, shaded "+~25%"; small "3.8% (Apr 2026)" flag at the end]

⏱ **VO:** But here's the stat that rewires how you see every headline: the people who run this machine — remember the dial from the Fed video? — have an official target for inflation. And that target is not zero.
It's two percent. A year. Every year. Forever.

[EDIT: full stop. Silence. Zoom-punch:]
[TEXT: "Target: +2%. Forever."]

**VO:** Two percent compounds. Run the machine at target — no crises, no mistakes, everything working *perfectly* — and prices double roughly every thirty-five years. Your grandkids will think your rent was a typo. On purpose.

[GFX: compounding curve — $100 basket doubling ~every 35 yrs: 2025 → 2060 → 2095]

## ACT 2 — WHY THE MACHINE FEARS REVERSE (3:00–5:35)

**VO:** Which raises the obvious question: why two? Why not zero — honest, stable, flat?
The answer is the deepest gear in this whole series: because the one thing this machine fears more than prices rising… is prices falling.

[TEXT: "The enemy isn't inflation. It's deflation."]

**VO:** Walk through it. If prices fall — and everyone *expects* them to keep falling — why buy anything today? Wait a month, it's cheaper. So spending stalls. Sales drop. Businesses cut wages, then jobs. Now people have less to spend, so prices fall further… and the economy eats itself in a loop. Economists watched it happen in the 1930s, and again in Japan for twenty years. Once the spiral starts, the dial from Video 2 stops working — you can't cut interest rates much below zero.

[GFX: the money-lungs from Video 1 exhaling and *not* inhaling back; spiral diagram: wait → cut → less money → wait harder]
[B-ROLL: 1930s breadline archival · Japanese empty shopping arcade footage]

**VO:** And there's a quieter reason — the one that connects every machine we've opened. Remember: almost all money is *loaned* into existence. Which means almost everyone — households, companies, the government itself — owes money. Inflation quietly shrinks debts: you repay with dollars that matter less. Deflation does the reverse — it makes every debt *heavier* while paychecks shrink. A system built entirely on debt cannot survive falling prices.

⏱ [EDIT: thesis on black:]
[TEXT: "A debt-based system must inflate. Slowly, forever."]

**VO:** So the machine is tuned to a ratchet: climb at two percent, never reverse. Not a conspiracy — a design constraint. And the strangest part? That sacred two percent number wasn't handed down by economic law. It was more or less improvised — it first appeared as an official target in New Zealand, around 1990, worked well enough, and the entire world copied it. The global speed limit for the price of everything started as one small country's round number.

[B-ROLL: New Zealand parliament archival; world map lighting up as central banks adopt "2%"]

## ACT 3 — THE STORY: "TRANSITORY" (5:35–8:00) ⏱

**VO:** Now watch the machine straining in real time. Twenty twenty-one. The pandemic money — trillions typed into being, remember — meets broken supply chains. Prices start moving. And the people at the dial say one word, over and over: *transitory.* Temporary. It'll pass. Don't worry about it.

[B-ROLL: 2021 press conferences; the word "TRANSITORY" repeating in caption pile-up]
[EDIT: *Pulse* cue]

**VO:** By June twenty twenty-two, inflation hits nine point one percent — the fastest in forty years. The word "transitory" is quietly retired. The dial gets cranked at the fastest pace since the nineteen-eighties. And it *works* — the climbing slows, from nine, to five, to three-ish.
Victory laps all around. Except — look at your receipt. Every one of those percentages *stacked.* Nine percent, then five, then three isn't a round trip. It's a staircase. The fastest staircase in four decades, and you're still standing on top of it, paying the new floor.

[GFX: staircase builds — each year's inflation a permanent step; a figure standing on top holding groceries; altimeter reads "+~25% vs 2019"]
[TEXT: "Disinflation still means: keep the new prices."]

**VO:** And here's the beat almost no one includes: the machine will never try to undo it. No central banker will ever aim for negative inflation to "give back" 2021. The spiral risk says they can't. The debt math says they won't. The official plan — stated out loud, in documents — is that the new prices are permanent, and we resume the two percent climb from here.

⏱ [EDIT: music out. One line, black:]
[TEXT: "The plan is the ratchet."]

## ACT 4 — LIVING ON A RATCHET (8:00–9:50)

**VO:** So you're standing on a staircase that only goes up. Four moves. ⏱

[EDIT: *Warm* cue]

**VO:** One: re-price your raise. If your pay rose less than inflation this year, you took a pay cut — politely, invisibly. The number to bring to a negotiation isn't "what am I worth plus a bit." It's CPI *plus* a bit. That's not greed; that's staying still.
Two: get on the right side of the ratchet. Inflation punishes cash and rewards long, fixed-rate debt — a fixed mortgage payment stays frozen while everything around it inflates, which is why homeowners quietly win the game renters are forced to play.
Three: never store years of savings in the machine's raw material. Cash is for emergencies and near-term plans; anything longer needs to at least chase the climb — remember the yield lever from Video 1.
Four: when you hear "inflation is back to normal," translate it: *the staircase is climbing at the designed speed again.* Normal was never flat.

[GFX: checklist ×4 — CPI+ negotiation card · frozen mortgage payment vs inflating rent column · cash melting on a shelf vs yield · staircase at steady 2% grade]

## CLOSE + SEQUEL LOOP (9:50–10:30)

**VO:** Four machines in: your bank lends your money into existence. The Fed prices it. A scoring machine prices *you.* And a ratchet makes sure the total only turns one way.
Which leaves a question: if prices only ever climb, and paychecks always lag… how is everyone still buying things? The answer is a fifth machine — the newest one, built into every checkout button, that splits any price into four painless pieces and calls it "not debt." It's growing faster than credit cards ever did, and almost none of it shows up on the radar you just learned about. That's the next video.
No hype. Just the gears.
