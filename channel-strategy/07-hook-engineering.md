# Phase 7: Hook Engineering — Five Openings, One Winner

**Goal:** maximize retention through 0:30 (target ≥ 70% of viewers still watching) and emotional investment through 1:00.

The script's original drafting-room hook was a straight claim-restate ("Your account balance is a number the bank owes you..."). Below, five complete rewrites in different styles, then the verdict. **The winner has already been inserted into `05-script.md`.**

---

## V1 — "The Locked Door" (dare / thought-experiment) ✅ WINNER

> Tomorrow morning, walk into your bank and ask for everything in your account. In cash. They'll probably give it to you.
> Now imagine everyone at your branch does the same thing, on the same day.
> The doors close by noon. Not because the bank is broken — because it was never holding your money in the first place.

**Why it works:** second-person imperative puts the viewer *inside* the scenario within 3 seconds; "they'll probably give it to you" is a false-reassurance beat that makes the reversal ("doors close by noon") land harder; final line restates the title claim *stronger* — packaging promise honored at 0:15.

## V2 — "The Story Cold Open" (narrative in-media-res)

> On a Wednesday in March, the sixteenth-largest bank in America was fine.
> By Friday, it did not exist.
> Forty-two billion dollars tried to walk out the door in one day — and what killed that bank is sitting inside your checking account right now.

**Why it works:** true-crime cadence, concrete numbers. **Cost:** spends the SVB story in the hook — it's the act-3 centerpiece, and burning it at 0:00 flattens the retention trough defense.

## V3 — "The Receipt" (prop / personal object)

> This number — your account balance — is one of the most convincing illusions ever built.
> It looks like money you have. It's actually money you've *lent*. To a bank. At almost zero percent. Without ever being asked.
> And what they're doing with it right now is the strangest business on Earth.

**Why it works:** instantly personal, pairs beautifully with thumbnail B. **Cost:** reveals the "you're the lender" twist — the emotional payoff currently earned at 2:40 — inside the first 15 seconds. Too much, too early.

## V4 — "The Confession" (authority / insider frame)

> Banks have a number they don't advertise: the percentage of your deposit they're required to keep on hand, in case you want it back.
> That number, in America, since 2020… is zero.
> Not ten percent, like you learned in school. Zero. And that's the *least* surprising thing you'll learn today.

**Why it works:** the 0% stat is genuinely shocking and verifiable — great comment bait. **Cost:** leads with abstraction (a percentage) rather than a scene; "least surprising thing" is a strong escalator but the opening beat is informational, not visceral. Also burns the 1:45 zoom-punch moment.

## V5 — "The Contrarian Flip" (misconception attack)

> Everything you picture when you think of a bank — the vault, the stacks of bills, your money sitting safely in a drawer with your name on it — none of it exists.
> There is no drawer. There are no stacks. There's a spreadsheet, a promise, and a 300-year-old machine running on pure belief.
> Today, we open the machine.

**Why it works:** strong imagery-demolition, brand-aligned ("open the machine"). **Cost:** stays abstract — nothing *happens* to anyone; "running on pure belief" pre-reveals the act-3 thesis (bank runs = belief failure).

---

## Verdict & Rationale

**Winner: V1 "The Locked Door."** It is the only version that (a) makes the viewer an actor rather than a listener, (b) honors the title claim harder within 15 seconds, and (c) reveals *nothing* — it proves the claim's stakes without explaining a single mechanism. Every open question at 0:35 is still open. V2 is the runner-up and its material stays exactly where it is (act 3); V4's 0% stat stays as the 1:45 pattern-interrupt.

## Early-Reveal / Drag Flags (fixed in final script)

1. ~~Original draft opened with "your balance is a number the bank owes you"~~ — that's the reveal, not the hook. Moved to 0:25 post-scenario. ✅
2. Do **not** name SVB, the FDIC, or "fractional reserve" before 3:00 — jargon at the top reads as "lecture incoming" and sheds casual viewers.
3. The concession beat ("this isn't a scam") must stay ≤ 8 seconds — trust beat, not a disclaimer paragraph.

## First-Minute Engineering Spec

- **First visual change: 0:03** — cut from bank doors to hands-counting-cash on "In cash." Never hold the opening shot past 4 seconds.
- **First pattern interrupt: 0:12–0:15** — hard cut to black + audio dropout on "by noon." (Silence is the interrupt; every finance video is wall-to-wall music, so absence = attention.)
- **Second interrupt: 0:28** — balance-dissolving-to-IOU graphic; first motion-graphic moment signals "this channel shows, not tells."
- **Emotional through-line 0:00–1:00:** unease (my money?) → false relief ("probably give it to you") → sharpened unease ("by noon") → intrigue ("strangest machine ever built") → trust ("this isn't a scam, it's 300 years old") → curiosity locked for act 1.
- **0:35 checkpoint:** viewer must be able to answer *what question this video answers* ("where IS my money?") but not the answer itself.
